Some of my best ideas arrive when I am not looking for them. The shower is the classic case. No phone, no music, just warm water and enough quiet for a thought to surface. A good idea floats up, sharp and clear. And just as fast, it is gone.

We move too fast to catch them. The day is loops of tasks, tabs, and timelines, and even when we stop working we do not really stop. We scroll, consume, react. That is motion without meaning, and in the blur the small things slip away. Ideas rarely shout. They show up only when there is room for them, and room is the first thing a busy week takes away.

## The shower fix

For weeks I had a problem I could not solve cleanly. I wanted to share a Postman collection with my team: versioned, free, no vendor lock-in. Postman's free tier did not cover team collaboration the way I needed, and I did not want our requests sitting in their cloud. Every time I came back to it I hit the same wall.

Then one evening in the shower it landed. Use the REST Client extension for VS Code. Write the requests as plain `.http` files, version them in a GitHub repo, let the team clone and run them. No Postman, no cloud, ours. It solved a problem that had nagged me for weeks, and it surfaced only because I gave it room.

## The cost of not catching them

Plenty of ideas have slipped through. One on a walk, one as I fell asleep. I told myself I would remember. I did not, and all that was left was the sense that something good had been there.

For a while I stopped writing them down at all. I was focused and efficient, checking boxes, and it felt fine. Then the work started to feel generic, like I was running tasks instead of making anything. Lose enough ideas and a quieter problem sets in: you start to believe they were never worth keeping, and you stop trusting your own instincts. That is the real cost, and it is why I went back to writing every idea down.

## The system

It is not complicated. When an idea shows up, I capture it right away, usually in Obsidian, sometimes a voice note or a sticky note. Quick, raw, unfinished. Once it is written it is safe. I can develop it later, or drop it, but I will not lose it.

Not every idea is good, and that is not the point. They are signals about what I care about and what still pulls at me. When I show up for them, more of them show up.

So make space, stay open, and write it down. Ideas do not owe us anything, but if you are paying attention, they tend to stay long enough to matter.
